# Summary of Findings

Across all three testers, a few strong themes showed up. Everyone who tested the game understood the cooking stages, so the core mechanic is straightforward and does not confuse the players. Where players struggled was not the basic game, it was the engagement and immersion. Every tester independently said the difficulty curve is too low or too predictable. The fixed timing creates a routine that becomes trivial after a few pancakes, so the game loses tension quickly. All three suggested adding systems that create variation or challenge: faster timers, random cooking timers, or customer orders.

Another clear pattern is that the audio feedback works, especially the sizzle and flip sounds. Multiple people said it helped them understand the state of the pancake. But they also said that there was a missing piece: visual feedback for scooping batter. One tester mentioned that without the graphic on the ladle, the scoop action felt empty because sound was not enough.

The one thing I loved was that each tester had something they found fun about the game. Every tester focused on a different “most fun” action: flipping, stacking, or scooping. That makes me know that the active, animated actions feel rewarding. The only other thing was that the dragging mechanic feels like busy work. Dragging the pancake to the plate was called out as boring by more than one tester. I think I might experiment with clicking on the pancake for it to move to the plate instead of having to manually drag.

All testers agree that there needs to be a reward put into the game, like orders, goals, rewards, or something that builds toward a challenge.
